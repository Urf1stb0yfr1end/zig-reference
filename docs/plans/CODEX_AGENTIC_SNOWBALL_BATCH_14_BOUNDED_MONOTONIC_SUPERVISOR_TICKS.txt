PHASE 3 — DEFINE A FINITE RE-ARM POLICY

Choose the smallest conventional policy that can produce more than one real timer interrupt and terminate exactly.

Use a small compiled expected tick count. Prefer enough events to prove repetition rather than a second isolated one-shot, while keeping execution quick and bounded. A value such as 4 is a reasonable hypothesis, but derive the final constant from reliable execution rather than treating this suggestion as sacred.

Each expected timer delivery must:

1. arrive through the existing supervisor trap vector;
2. classify as interrupt bit 1 / supervisor timer cause 5;
3. preserve interrupt sepc semantics;
4. capture monotonic time evidence;
5. increment a bounded tick count exactly once;
6. either program the next future deadline or, for the final tick, perform explicit final neutralization;
7. restore context;
8. return with sret.

The re-arm policy must be explicit.

Acceptable shapes include programming each next deadline from a newly observed monotonic time plus a bounded interval, or another conventional relationship justified by evidence.

Do not make unproven claims about long-term periodic drift, phase lock, frequency accuracy, or scheduler quantum precision.

If the handler can observe a late deadline, define the bounded policy explicitly rather than allowing an immediate unbounded interrupt loop.

The final tick must end in an explicit neutral state, for example masking STIE and moving the programmed deadline out of the active execution window, if that remains the conventional evidenced mechanism.

No interrupt storm.
No infinite tick loop.
No periodic background activity after the proof phase.
